## The 5 real methodological pillars (each = one click-to-open graphic card)

1. **The Action-Oriented Approach (l'approche actionnelle)**
   - Learner as "social agent" who acts with intentionality to accomplish real-life tasks.
   - "Learners don't merely learn about the language — they live the language." (mirrors brand line "Live French. Live.")
   - Source: Piccardo, E. & North, B. (2019). *The Action-Oriented Approach: A Dynamic Vision of Language Education.* Bristol: Multilingual Matters.
   - Source: Council of Europe (2020). *CEFR Companion Volume.* Strasbourg: Council of Europe Publishing.

2. **Real Scenarios & Tasks (situated learning)**
   - Learning happens in authentic, real-life-oriented scenarios. Each unit anchors to a real French place/situation (this ties directly to the brand's Mapstr pedagogy — "French is learned where it is lived").
   - "Scenarios are blueprints for projects... each task implies the creation of some form of artefact." (Piccardo & North, 2019, p.272)
   - Backwards design: modules of 3–10 lessons working toward "can-do" learning outcomes.

3. **The Four Modes of Communication: Reception · Production · Interaction · Mediation**
   - CEFR moves beyond the old "four skills" to include interaction and mediation.
   - Mediation = the learner "creates bridges and helps construct or convey meaning" across people, cultures, and languages. (Council of Europe, 2020, p.90)
   - Source: North, B., Piccardo, E. & Goodier, T. (2020), CEFR Companion Volume authors.

4. **Communicative + Eclectic Instruction (with explicit guided structure)**
   - A balanced, mixed method: communicative interaction + meaningful action-based tasks + guided acquisition of French linguistic structures.
   - Empirically validated: learners on the mixed approach outperform traditional methods, especially in oral interaction. (FFL novice-learner methodology studies, CEFR A0→A1 progression)
   - Roots: Dewey, Vygotsky, Bruner (socio-constructivism); Bourguignon (2010) action-oriented design.

5. **Plurilingual & Pluricultural Competence (culture at the core)**
   - Language as a dynamic, creative process of "languaging" across cultures; culture is inseparable from language.
   - Supports the brand promise: "Culture speaks French." Certified to CEFR levels (A0 → A2.2 across the course ladder), aligned to DELF/DALF-style "can-do" assessment.
   - Source: Piccardo & North (2019), "Broadening the scope of language education: plurilingualism, mediation and collaborative learning."
