Capsule 01 · Fashion & Art

How France invented elegance — from Versailles to Chanel.

From Louis XIV to JR: how taste was manufactured by a king, set free by artists, and handed to the street.

The ten classes of Fashion & Art

Ten classes on the people who made French taste: a king who turned fashion into state policy, the painters who broke the rules, the couturiers who dressed the century.

Syllabus PDF
1
Fashion as state strategy
Louis XIV

Colbert, the silk of Lyon, the Gobelins: French elegance begins as industrial policy and soft power.

2
The eye set free
Claude Monet

Impressionism starts as an insult. What it did to painting, and how America bought Monet first.

3
Emotion over decoration
Auguste Rodin

The scandal of his early work, The Thinker, The Kiss — and Camille Claudel, finally restored.

4
The lesson of colour
Henri Matisse

The Fauve scandal, the discipline behind the joy, the cut-outs invented from a wheelchair.

5
The comfort revolution
Coco Chanel

Jersey, the little black dress, No. 5, the chain strap — and the documented shadow of the war years.

6
February 1947
Christian Dior

One collection relaunches a ruined industry. The New Look, its critics, and Catherine Dior, résistante.

7
The emancipation wardrobe
Yves Saint Laurent

Le Smoking, the trench, the safari jacket, luxury ready-to-wear — and the 1983 Met retrospective.

8
A 25-year alliance
Hubert de Givenchy

A young couturier, an unknown actress named Audrey Hepburn, and how a wardrobe builds a persona.

9
The rebel who opened the doors
Jean-Paul Gaultier

Sailor stripes, men in skirts, every body on the runway — decades before anyone said inclusion.

10
Art off the wall
JR

Monumental portraits on favelas, borders, prisons and the Louvre pyramid: participation as a medium.

Bread protected by decree, a cheese that is a battlefield, a name defended in court on five continents.

The ten classes of Gastronomy & Wine

Ten classes on what France chose to protect: the loaf, the butter, the cheese, the bubble, the truffle — and the laws, riots and treaties behind each of them.

Syllabus PDF
1
Bread as a right
The Baguette

The Décret Pain, UNESCO heritage in 2022 — and why Napoleon’s soldiers never carried one.

2
A tax you can taste
Salted Butter

The gabelle, the salt tax Brittany escaped: why butter is salted in Brittany and sweet in Normandy.

3
Raw milk against the world
Camembert

Two food cultures in a small round box: flavour against sanitary norms, farm to Great War symbol.

4
Defending a name
Champagne

The second fermentation, the riots of 1911, the birth of the AOC — and the long argument with California.

5
Affordable luxury, perfected
The Macaron

From convent biscuit to Parisian double-decker: the most efficient luxury marketing ever devised.

6
The cultural dare
Snails & Frogs

Roman farms to Burgundian butter, monastic loophole to Lyonnais delicacy — and the ecology of a trade.

7
The honest argument
Foie Gras

Heritage protected by law, the producers’ case and the welfare scientists’ case — no verdict handed down.

8
Paris, capital of craft
Chocolate

From the Spanish court to Bayonne’s Sephardic chocolatiers and on to the Parisian ateliers.

9
The diamond of Périgord
The Black Truffle

Why it cannot be farmed, why production collapsed, and why the pig was retired for the dog.

10
Peasant genius
Ratatouille

A poor man’s stew from the Provençal garden to Escoffier — and the chefs who rebuilt cooking around vegetables.

The ten classes of Cinema & Music

Ten classes on the screen and the song: the magician who invented special effects, the voice that conquered New York, the directors who rewrote the grammar of cinema.

Syllabus PDF
1
The magician of cinema
Georges Méliès

A conjuror invents special effects, loses everything, sells toys in a station — and is resurrected by Hollywood.

2
The icon of resilience
Édith Piaf

From the Paris streets to the conquest of America: tragedy turned into a universal language.

3
The singer who conquered America
Yves Montand

Son of Italian refugees, summit of the music hall, Signoret, the Marilyn scandal, redemption.

4
Songs as screenplays
Charles Aznavour

A methodical songwriter overcomes relentless criticism of his voice to become France itself.

5
Freedom, filmed
Bardot & the Nouvelle Vague

A new feminine myth and a band of young critics with hand-held cameras rewrite cinema.

6
The genius of provocation
Serge Gainsbourg

A failed painter turns his fractures into a signature and masters the art of scandal.

7
Cold magnetism
Delon, Melville & the polar

The silence of Le Samouraï — a star who never went to Hollywood, yet is everywhere in it.

8
The yéyé explosion
Hallyday & Hardy

The rock animal and the melancholy icon: a generation imports American rock and sends something new back.

9
The imaginary Paris
Amélie & Yann Tiersen

Fairy-tale Montmartre, the casting gamble, and the accordion restored to respectability.

10
Sixty years of Oscars
Hollywood’s French accent

From Maurice Jarre to Alexandre Desplat: the French composers the Academy keeps rewarding.

Questions

Questions before you join.

Do I need to speak French?

No. The Culture Capsules are taught in English by bilingual speakers, with the French names and places pronounced as the French do. No prior knowledge is assumed.

What exactly is a capsule?

Ten live one-hour classes on one theme — Fashion & Art, Gastronomy & Wine, or Cinema & Music — on Zoom, led by a signature speaker, followed by 15–20 minutes of your questions.

How do the payments work?

Three monthly payments. One capsule is $99 a month ($297 in total). Two capsules are $178 a month — $89 per capsule, $534 in total. All three capsules are $189 a month — just $63 per capsule, $567 in total instead of $891. Nothing more to pay.

How does the CAPSULE20 code work?

Enter CAPSULE20 in checkout and press Apply to receive 20% off one, two or all three capsules. It is never applied automatically. Discounted monthly payments are rounded to the nearest whole dollar, and the total is the sum of the three payments. Checkout shows the updated amount due today, remaining payments, total payable and savings before you proceed.

Do I need a language course to join?

No. The Capsules are sold independently of the language courses. Many participants join for the culture alone; others add a capsule alongside their French course.

Where can I read the full programme?

Open any capsule above to see its ten classes, or download the complete syllabus as a PDF.
