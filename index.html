The Science of Our Method

Built on the action-oriented tradition of the Sorbonne

The French Atelier method is built upon the modern science of language acquisition — the action-oriented, communicative tradition advanced by applied linguists at the Sorbonne and codified in the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). Live French. Live. — because learners don't merely learn about the language, they live it.

“Learners don't merely learn about the language — they live the language.”

You are treated as a social agent who acts with intention to accomplish real-life tasks. Every lesson moves you toward doing something real in French — ordering, asking, negotiating, connecting — rather than memorising rules in the abstract. This is the principle behind our promise: Live French. Live.

Source: Piccardo, E. & North, B. (2019). The Action-Oriented Approach: A Dynamic Vision of Language Education. Multilingual Matters · Council of Europe (2020), CEFR Companion Volume. Read more ↗

“Scenarios are blueprints for projects… each task implies the creation of some form of artefact.”

Learning happens inside authentic, real-life scenarios. Each unit anchors to a real French place or situation — the same pedagogy as our living map of France: French is learned where it is lived. Using backwards design, modules of 3–10 lessons work toward clear “can-do” outcomes.

Source: Piccardo & North (2019), p.272. Action-Oriented Approach overview ↗

Mediation: the learner “creates bridges and helps construct or convey meaning” across people, cultures and languages.

The CEFR moves beyond the old “four skills” to include interaction and mediation. Our small live groups of 8–10 are designed precisely for this — you don't just receive and produce French, you interact in it and learn to bridge meaning across cultures.

Source: North, B., Piccardo, E. & Goodier, T. — Council of Europe (2020), CEFR Companion Volume, p.90. European Centre for Modern Languages ↗

Learners on the mixed approach outperform traditional methods — especially in oral interaction.

A balanced, mixed method: communicative interaction, meaningful action-based tasks, and the guided acquisition of French linguistic structures. Its roots run through Dewey, Vygotsky and Bruner (socio-constructivism) and Bourguignon's action-oriented design — rigorous structure in service of real speaking.

Roots: Dewey, Vygotsky, Bruner; Bourguignon (2010). Empirically validated across FFL novice-learner methodology studies (CEFR A0→A1). CASLT ↗

Culture speaks French. Language is a dynamic, creative process of “languaging” across cultures.

Culture is inseparable from language. Our course ladder is certified to CEFR levels (A0 → A2.2) and aligned to DELF/DALF-style “can-do” assessment — so cultural fluency and measurable progress advance together.

Source: Piccardo & North (2019), “Broadening the scope of language education: plurilingualism, mediation and collaborative learning.” Multilingual Matters ↗
