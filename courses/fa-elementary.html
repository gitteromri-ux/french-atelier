The full syllabus

20 lessons. One journey.

FA Elementary follows the great Atlantic diagonal: from Orléans and the Renaissance gardens of Villandry, through Chenonceau and the terraces of Tours, down to the vineyards and quays of Bordeaux, the Dune du Pilat, and on to the Basque coast — Bayonne, Biarritz, Saint-Jean-de-Luz and the red-pepper village of Espelette.

01
Arrivée à Orléans

In Orléans you reactivate introductions and learn to chain a longer exchange, linking several questions and answers into a real conversation. The can-do: sustain a short multi-turn dialogue.

Orléans
02
Dans les jardins de Villandry

Among the Renaissance gardens of Villandry you describe a place in detail and express admiration, expanding your stock of qualifying adjectives. You leave able to describe and react to a beautiful setting.

Renaissance gardens of Villandry
03
Au château de Chenonceau

At Chenonceau you ask for and give information on a guided visit, understanding a simple commentary about the past. The outcome: follow a cultural tour and ask informed questions.

Château de Chenonceau
04
Sur les terrasses de Tours

On the terraces of Tours you order, recommend and react to recommendations, justifying choices with parce que. You can now give and respond to a simple recommendation.

Terraces of Tours
05
Un déjeuner en guinguette

At a Loire guinguette you talk about food and conviviality, narrate a recent meal, and use the passé composé for the first time. The can-do: recount a short past experience.

Loire guinguette
06
Dans le parc de Poitiers

In the park of Poitiers you describe leisure and habits, contrasting past and present with simple time markers. You leave able to compare what you used to do and do now.

Park of Poitiers
07
Chez le marchand de vin

In a Bordeaux wine merchant's you ask for advice, express preference and quantity, and complete a guided purchase. The outcome: handle an advised, specialised purchase.

Bordeaux vineyards & wine merchants
08
Au Grand Théâtre de Bordeaux

Before the Grand Théâtre you book seats, discuss a programme, and express anticipation, using the near future and opinion phrases. You can now organise an evening at a venue.

Grand Théâtre de Bordeaux
09
Le long des quais de Bordeaux

On the waterfront quays you give directions across a city and describe an itinerary, narrating a short walk. The can-do: explain a route through a place you know.

Bordeaux waterfront quays
10
À la Cité du Vin

Inside La Cité du Vin you describe a process and sequence steps with connectors, talking through how something is made. You leave able to explain a simple process in order.

La Cité du Vin
11
Au musée des Beaux-Arts

At the Musée des Beaux-Arts you describe a work of art — its subject, colours and the feeling it gives you — your first guided aesthetic description. The outcome: describe and react to a painting.

Musée des Beaux-Arts
12
Au sommet de la Dune du Pilat

Atop Europe's tallest dune you narrate an excursion in the past tense, describing the effort and the view. You can now tell the story of an outing.

Dune du Pilat
13
Aux ports ostréicoles

In the oyster ports of Gujan-Mestras you talk about regional produce, order a tasting, and ask about origin and method. The can-do: discuss and order a local speciality.

Oyster ports of Gujan-Mestras
14
À la cathédrale de Bayonne

Before Bayonne's Gothic cathedral you describe architecture and history simply, asking and answering questions about a monument. You leave able to talk about a historic building.

Bayonne Gothic cathedral
15
Au Rocher de la Vierge

At Biarritz's Rocher de la Vierge you describe a landscape and the weather together, expressing impressions and comparisons. The outcome: paint a place in words with feeling.

Biarritz Rocher de la Vierge
16
L'ascension de la Rhune

On the little train up La Rhune you narrate a journey step by step and express physical sensation, consolidating the passé composé. You can now recount a trip with sequence and reaction.

La Rhune
17
Dans le port de Saint-Jean-de-Luz

In the harbour of Saint-Jean-de-Luz you plan a day with someone, negotiate options, and reach agreement, justifying preferences. The can-do: organise a shared day out by discussion.

Saint-Jean-de-Luz harbour
18
À Espelette

In the red-pepper capital of Espelette you talk about taste, intensity and cooking, comparing flavours with simple comparatives. You leave able to describe and compare tastes.

Espelette (red pepper capital)
19
Raconter le voyage

You narrate the whole Atlantic journey, chaining several past events with connectors and reactions — your most extended account yet. The outcome: tell a multi-part story of a trip.

Bordeaux waterfront quays
20
Bilan — décrire, justifier, raconter

Your final unit weaves description, justification and past narration into one fluent exchange — a best-of review confirming you can now produce semi-guided language across real situations.

La Cité du Vin

Questions & answers

Everything you need to know

Including how it works, the certificate, and what it costs.

FA Elementary is built for learners at CEFR A1.2 entering at A1.2 and finishing around A2.1. It assumes you've reached the level just below and want to consolidate and grow. Lessons are live, in small groups, and led by native certified teachers broadcasting from France.

You attend 20 live 85 min sessions in groups of 8–10 learners. Every unit is a real communicative task anchored in a real French location, so the language attaches to a place and a purpose. Recordings are available so you never miss a step.

Yes. On completion you receive a CEFR-aligned certificate of achievement issued by Acadomia, France's #1 private learning institute. Our framework follows the same Common European levels recognised across French academia and employers — the standard of reference used by institutions including the Sorbonne.

The French Atelier is presented by Acadomia — founded in 1989 and France's #1 private learning institute — in partnership with the eTeacher award-winning live online learning platform. You learn from native, certified French teachers, supported around the clock by Juliane, our 24/7 AI tutor.

Absolutely. An education advisor will help you place into the right course and build a plan around your schedule and goals.

FA courses run from $42 a week on the annual plan — $840 in total for all 20 live lessons — or pay monthly from $56 a week. Pricing is always discussed with your advisor so it fits your situation — there are no hidden fees, and a $100 learning credit is available on completion.
