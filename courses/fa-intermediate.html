Course Overview

From the calanques to the champagne cellars

For graduates of FA Elementary. By the end of FA Intermediate, you can produce language autonomously, give and defend an opinion, negotiate a compromise, handle unexpected situations, narrate past events with nuance, and engage in intercultural comparisons.

FA Intermediate covers three very different corners of France in a single immersive journey. It begins in Marseille: the ancient Vieux-Port, the colourful alleyways of the Panier, the calanques of the Provence coast, the neighbourhood of L'Estaque, the Vallon des Auffes, the MuCEM, and a viewpoint across the Alps toward Mont-Blanc. It then heads to the mountains: Chamonix, with its glaciers, its legendary rack railway up to the Mer de Glace, and the vertiginous Aiguille du Midi at 3,842 metres. The journey continues east to Alsace and Champagne: the medieval half-timbered streets of Strasbourg's Petite France, the Christmas market on the Place Broglie, the Gothic cathedral in pink sandstone, a traditional Winstub supper, then Reims with its royal cathedral, its indoor market, the underground chalk cellars where Champagne matures, and a closing dinner reflecting on French and world cultures.

Full Syllabus

20 units. Marseille to Reims.

From the calanques to the Gothic cathedral of Strasbourg to a closing dinner in the champagne country. Debate, negotiate, nuance, reflect.

01
Inviter / proposer une sortie
Invite someone out using et si on allait au théâtre ?, agree on a time and place (on peut se retrouver à 19h devant le métro), and confirm warmly. Produce a spontaneous, unscripted 10-turn exchange.
02
Refuser poliment + proposer une alternative
Decline an invitation using merci, j'aimerais bien, mais je ne peux pas ce soir, give a brief reason, and immediately offer a concrete alternative (on peut plutôt y aller samedi ?). Maintain a consistent tu / vous register throughout.
03
Négocier + conclure un compromis
Raise a constraint (c'est un peu cher), propose a practical concession (si on prend les places au fond, c'est moins cher), and close with a clear joint decision (OK, on fait comme ça). Practice structured negotiation over at least 12 turns.
04
Exprimer une préférence culturelle + comparer
Share a preference about social customs using moi, je préfère…, justify it once (parce que), and invite comparison (et dans ton pays, on fait comment ?). Discover the cultural codes around la bise and greetings in France.
05
Exprimer une gêne + demander conseil
Express uncertainty about a social situation (je ne sais pas si on fait la bise ou pas…), ask for the other person's preference, and propose a comfortable alternative (si tu veux, on peut se serrer la main).
06
Recommander + micro-récit au passé
Open with a short past-tense anecdote (hier, je suis allé(e) au cinéma), make a recommendation (je te conseille ce film), justify it, introduce a nuance (en revanche…), and invite the other person to go.
07
Donner un avis + voter entre deux options
Share a personal opinion about a cultural event (je l'ai adorée : je la trouve très drôle), justify it, compare two options, and reach a joint decision. Practice pronominal agreement with direct object pronouns.
08
Donner un avis + comparer deux œuvres
Express two opinions on works of art using j'aime bien… / je trouve…, compare them (celui-là est plus sombre, mais il est beau), and ask et toi, tu préfères lequel ?. Discover key works of French art.
09
Organiser un parcours + gérer un imprévu
Plan a three-stage visit (d'abord… ensuite… après…), handle an unexpected closure (mince, la salle est fermée… on fait quoi ?), and agree on a plan B. Practice improvised decision-making in a cultural setting.
10
Comparer + nuancer un avis
Compare two cultural experiences (l'opéra est plus long et plus impressionnant), introduce nuance with mais / en revanche / au final, and state a clear personal preference. Explore the French performing arts scene.

Chamonix · Alsace · Champagne

11
Recommander un endroit + se repérer
Express a preference for a neighbourhood, handle a moment of disorientation (excuse-moi, je suis un peu perdu(e)…), and make a local recommendation (je te conseille ce café, parce qu'il est sympa).
12
Nuancer un avis + défendre un choix
Assert a position (pour moi, c'est plus intéressant), acknowledge the counterargument (oui, mais c'est aussi plus difficile), and defend a practical decision using concessive connectors.
13
Proposer + comparer deux destinations
Propose two travel destinations, compare them on one criterion, justify your preference (parce que), and reach a joint decision. Practice the vocabulary of French regions and travel.
14
Organiser un programme + gérer un désaccord + un imprévu
Negotiate a 3-step itinerary with a diverging view, use concessive connectors (certes…, mais / même si / pourtant), then handle an unexpected problem (notre hôtel n'est plus disponible) and agree on a plan B together.
15
Raconter une expérience (week-end) + relancer
Narrate a past weekend in 4 sentences using the passé composé, express a reaction or emotion, and hand the floor back (et toi, tu as fait quoi ?). Discover the traditions and landscapes of French regions.
16
Comparer + recommander selon un critère
Compare two destinations on multiple criteria, recommend one conditionally (je te recommande la Bretagne si tu aimes la nature), and introduce a counterpoint (en revanche, la Provence est mieux pour la chaleur).
17
Acheter au marché + négocier un prix
Request a specific quantity of a product, ask a quality question, and attempt a soft negotiation (vous me faites un petit prix ?). Close the interaction politely whether or not the discount is granted. Discover the culture of French outdoor markets.
18
Signaler un problème + demander une solution
Signal a problem at a restaurant or shop calmly and factually (excusez-moi, il y a un petit problème…), request a remedy (vous pouvez le réchauffer, s'il vous plaît ?), and conclude the interaction once the issue is resolved.
19
Raconter un événement culturel + relancer
Narrate a past experience of a French national celebration in 4 sentences, use a time marker (l'année dernière, j'ai regardé…), describe the atmosphere, and invite the other person to share their own experience. Discover the traditions of le 14 juillet.
20
Comparer deux cultures + exprimer un avis nuancé
Compare a French tradition with its equivalent in your own culture, give a personal opinion (en France, je trouve ça plus festif), justify it, and introduce a counterpoint (en revanche…). A full consolidation of FA3 language: past narration, comparison, nuance, and intercultural reflection.
