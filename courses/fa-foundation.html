The full syllabus

20 lessons. One journey.

FA Foundation unfolds entirely in Paris over one long, luminous weekend. Each unit is anchored in a real landmark — the Eiffel Tower, a bakery in the 16th, the legendary Bouillon Chartier, the Palais-Royal gardens, Shakespeare & Co, Montmartre, the Canal Saint-Martin — so the language attaches itself to a place you can picture.

01
Se présenter à la Tour Eiffel

At the foot of the Eiffel Tower on the Champ-de-Mars, you learn to greet, exchange names, and propose tu with the verb s'appeler. You decode the cultural codes of a first French meeting — the handshake, the bonjour, the eye contact. By the end you can introduce yourself to a stranger and respond when they introduce themselves.

Eiffel Tower & Champ-de-Mars
02
Épeler et vérifier une information

In a bakery in the 16th arrondissement you practise the French alphabet and the sounds that trip up beginners. You ask how a word is spelled, spell your own name back, and confirm or correct using simple clarification strategies. The can-do outcome: reliably give and check spelling out loud.

Bakery, 16th arrondissement
03
Demander son chemin

Lost near the Alma bridge, you learn excusez-moi and c'est où ? to ask the way. You understand short replies — left, right, straight on — and pick up vocabulary for the public places around you. You leave able to ask for and follow simple directions on a real street.

Classic café near the Alma bridge
04
Réparer et commander une boisson

At a classic café you signal a communication breakdown, ask someone to repeat or slow down, and place your first simple order. These repair phrases are the safety net that keeps every later conversation afloat. You can now order a drink and recover when you don't understand.

Classic café near the Alma bridge
05
Commander [1/5] — Je voudrais… c'est combien ?

Inside the bustling Bouillon Chartier you order food and drink with je voudrais, ask the price, and handle numbers 0–39. You taste a slice of working-class Parisian dining culture. The outcome: place a basic order and understand a simple price.

Bouillon Chartier
06
Commander [2/5] — Autre chose ?

Still at Chartier, you respond to Autre chose ? and C'est tout ?, add items to your order, and stretch your numbers to 69. You read a real brasserie menu and choose with confidence. You can extend an order and react to the waiter's prompts.

Bouillon Chartier
07
Proposer une sortie simple

In the Palais-Royal gardens you use être and the pronoun y to suggest an outing, then accept or decline. You reach a simple shared decision with a friend. The can-do: propose a plan and agree on it.

Palais-Royal gardens
08
Accepter, refuser, préférer

Walking through the Tuileries you use je préfère to choose between two options and refuse politely without offence. You learn the small phrases of French agreement and gentle disagreement. You can now state a preference and settle a friendly choice.

Tuileries
09
Échanger un numéro de téléphone

On the Pont des Arts you ask for and give a phone number, then verify it with c'est ça ?, practising numbers 70–99 and French mobile conventions. You leave able to exchange and confirm a number aloud.

Pont des Arts
10
Commander [3/5] — Payer par carte

Back at a café you express hunger and thirst with j'ai faim / soif, order, and pay by card from start to finish. This is your first complete, self-contained transaction in French. You can run a café interaction end to end.

Classic café near the Alma bridge
11
Acheter un billet, demander un prix

At the Panthéon you buy a ticket, ask the price, and pay, consolidating je voudrais and payment vocabulary. You discover the role monuments and museums play in French civic life. The outcome: buy entry to a cultural site unaided.

Panthéon
12
Donner son email et son adresse

In the Jardin du Luxembourg you exchange email and street addresses using the possessives mon, ma, spelling the tricky parts and confirming details. You can now share and note down contact information clearly.

Jardin du Luxembourg
13
Parler de son origine et d'où l'on habite

At Shakespeare & Co you use venir de and habiter à to say where you are from and where you live, and to introduce a third person. You leave able to talk simply about your origins and home.

Shakespeare & Co
14
Gérer un imprévu

On the Coulée Verte you face a closed venue and learn to propose an alternative plan and decide on the spot, drilling nous sommes / ils sont and booking vocabulary. The can-do: handle a small surprise and keep the day moving.

Coulée Verte
15
Fixer un rendez-vous

At Bastille you propose a time and date, negotiate with ça va ?, confirm, and close the exchange — telling the time and using the days of the week in context. You can now make and confirm a simple appointment.

Bastille
16
Commander [4/5] — Réactivation

Back in a brasserie you revisit the ordering sequence at speed: three ways to answer Autre chose ?, ordering one or two items, and closing politely. This consolidation builds the automaticity real conversation demands.

Bouillon Chartier
17
Commander [5/5] — Je prends… l'addition

In Montmartre near the Place du Tertre you use je prends, react when an item is unavailable, and ask for the bill — a fluent, complete restaurant interaction. You leave able to dine out and pay from start to finish.

Montmartre & Place du Tertre
18
Exprimer et justifier une préférence

On the terraces of Montmartre you choose between two options, say je préfère, and justify with parce que c'est + adjectif, practising adjective agreement and opinion exchange. The outcome: give a short reasoned preference.

Montmartre & Place du Tertre
19
Arrivée — présentation et orientation [1/2]

Along the Canal Saint-Martin you chain two micro-tasks — introducing yourself and asking the way — adding a repair strategy if needed. This best-of review of units 1–3 proves how far you've come.

Canal Saint-Martin
20
Commander, payer, réparer, conclure [2/2]

Your final unit by the canal: complete a full ordering-and-payment scenario at speed, using at least one correct formula and closing politely. A best-of review of units 4–18 — and the confirmation that you can now survive a weekend in Paris in French.

Canal Saint-Martin
