Les petites histoires qui font la grande Histoire

Each capsule begins with something you think you know — the baguette, Chanel, Piaf — and works outward into the history hiding behind the cliché.

A salt tax that explains why butter is salted in Brittany. A laboratory sample number that becomes the world’s best-selling perfume. A bitter French song about a couple falling out of love that Frank Sinatra turned into the American anthem of self-determination. Every subject is tied back to what binds France to the United States: the collectors, the soldiers, the films, the songs and the arguments the two countries have shared for two hundred years.

Pack 01 · Fashion & Art · French aesthetics

The most visited museum on earth is in Paris: 8.7 million people walked into the Louvre last year.

French elegance is not a gift of national temperament: it is a construction, mixing technical rigor with freedom of spirit. This pack shows taste being manufactured by a king, prised away from authority by artists, and finally handed to the street — from Versailles to the walls of the world.

Pack 02 · Gastronomy & Wine · Terroir on the plate

France bakes more than 6 billion baguettes a year — and the law defines what may be called bread.

For the French, eating is a political, historical and identitarian act. A loaf is protected by decree, a cheese is a battlefield, a name is defended in court across five continents. You arrive expecting recipes and leave with an argument about what a country chooses to protect.

Pack 03 · Cinema & Music · Screens and songs

Cinema was born in a Paris café: 28 December 1895, the Grand Café, about forty paying viewers.

French cinema and French song are two forms of popular literature: a country that gives its highest cultural status to the lyricist and the auteur, and that has spent a century exporting its faces, its stories and its melodies to America — often without America noticing where they came from.

Questions

Before you join.

Do I need to speak French?

No. The Culture Capsules are taught in English by bilingual speakers, with the French words, names and places pronounced as the French do. No prior knowledge is assumed.

What exactly is a capsule?

A pack of ten live one-hour conferences around a single theme — Fashion & Art, Gastronomy & Wine, or Cinema & Music — delivered on Zoom by a signature speaker and followed by 15–20 minutes of questions.

How do the payments work?

Each capsule is paid in three monthly payments. One capsule is $99 a month; two capsules are $89 per capsule a month ($178); all three are $79 per capsule a month ($237). The totals are $297, $534 and $711 over three months, and there is nothing more to pay.

How does the CAPSULE20 code work?

Enter CAPSULE20 at checkout on a single capsule and the price drops by 20%: $79.20 a month, $237.60 over three months. The code is valid for 7 days from the moment you claim it.

Do I need a language course to join?

No. The Capsules are sold independently of the language courses. Many participants join for the culture alone; others add a capsule alongside their French course.
