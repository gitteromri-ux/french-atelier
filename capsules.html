Power · Spectacle · Taste

Louis XIV: The Inventor of Luxury

Louis XIV invented luxury as power.

Before Versailles, magnificence was scattered. The Sun King gathered it, staged it, and turned it into the instrument of a nation. From the Hall of Mirrors to the etiquette of the court, he made taste itself a form of rule — and in doing so, wrote the first chapter of what the world still calls French luxury. In this capsule, we follow the language of grandeur: the words, gestures and rituals that turned a king into a sun.

Freedom · Modernity · The Body

The Chanel Revolution

Gabrielle invented luxury as freedom.

Two centuries after Louis XIV, a girl raised in the orphanage of Aubazine rewrote the rules he had set. Gabrielle Chanel took luxury off the pedestal and put it onto the moving body — jersey instead of corsets, black instead of excess, a wardrobe a woman could actually live in. She didn't decorate the modern woman; she invented her. This capsule traces the vocabulary of that revolution — the language of elegance, liberty, and the modern French silhouette.

60 minLive masterclass
1883–1971Gabrielle Chanel
